## Product intent

- E-sign for **Union Payroll**. Almost every contract is 3-way (**Contractor**, **Broker**, **Client**). A 4-way adds **Supplier (SPV)**. API keys remain `supplier` / `agency` / `company`, plus `spv`. Sign order is Contractor, Supplier (4-way only), Broker, Client.
- Spec: `docs/esign-data-model.pdf` (Postgres/GCS in the brief → SQLite + local disk here).
- The app header says **Contract Builder**. A contract is sent for a broker, and that broker’s cover is what the parties see on the document. Union Payroll (https://union-payroll.ltd/) is one broker. E Street Management (https://es.management/) is another. The header does not use a single broker’s logo. Accent `#0074FF`, Manrope, white-text primary buttons.

## Current product shape

1. **Brokers** — each broker holds legal details, the front cover, the back cover, and the brand SVG. A cover can be a template PDF with named text fields and placeholders such as `[Reference]`; the app fills and flattens it (`addTemplateCover`, generator `scripts/covers/es-management.mjs`). E Street Management’s covers work that way; Union Payroll’s are filled by layout detection. Brokers do not send email. They have no sending domain, from address, mailbox, VAT number, or signature. The **Compliance company** sends the contract; its introductory email names the broker it is sent on behalf of (`{{broker_name}}`, `{{compliance_name}}`, and `{{other_parties}}`, which follows 3-way or 4-way). The compliance company has no Companies House number. The due diligence pack is built and signed in the app, and is not emailed out for anyone to complete. The API still stores brokers as entities.
2. **Templates** — one **master contract** (always sent) + optional **contract appendices**. Same file controls as company brand files: **Open / Replace / Delete** (Are you sure?) plus **Add appendix** at the top of Contract appendices. Uploading a PDF detects Client / Broker / Contractor fields, and Supplier (SPV) when that word is on the page. Signing has jump-to.
3. **Start** — after login the only question is: working from the library, or sending a new contract? A new contract asks for the broker (from the saved list), the contract type, 3-way or 4-way, then the other parties (company, signatory, role, email). The library path picks a saved broker and contractor, then asks 3-way or 4-way and the remaining parties. Companies and templates stay available from a setup link, not the main navigation. Every party record stores company name, signatory, role (Director or Authorised signatory) and email. The contract carries a signature and a date for each party. The broker signs and dates the due diligence pack.
4. **Contracts** (API still uses envelope tables/routes; UI always says contract) — the due diligence pack is built in the app before the contract is issued. No party is emailed to complete it. Companies House filings (incorporation, change of name, registered office, PSC, officers) and a UK Sanctions List check are fetched from the company number for the contractor and, on a 4-way, the supplier. Only one filing per fact is kept (the incorporation, each change of name, the latest AD01, notices for current PSCs only) and the pack attaches only its first page, the certificate or notice — never the IN01, model articles or PSC history. Staff attach only what cannot be fetched. Contractor: VAT certificate, Employers’ Liability, Public Liability, Professional Indemnity certificate, PI policy schedule and wording, bank statement for the account paying workers and HMRC, illustrative payslip, and a payroll chain map with PAYE/ERN confirmation. Pack and app wording stays short: checklists, not explanations. The pack is laid out like the worked example: the broker’s front cover with the pack details in its panel, branded headers, page numbers and the pack reference on every page (never “Doc ID”), bordered tables, check results Clear (green tick) / Flag (red cross) / N/A with “Reviewed by / Date” under each checks table, annexes numbered 1–n and titled “Annex n: Document, DD/MM/YYYY”, a Disclosures Part listing every Companies House change, and framed, captioned annex pages (`server/src/packLayout.js`). The v7 brief (`docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md` item 31) was written for the 4-way pack; by user decision its fixes apply to 3-way packs and the contract covers too, and its section 2 additions get a version without the SPV for 3-way packs. Each company’s annexes start with a divider page headed like its Part (e.g. “Part D — Supplier (SPV): ARC RISK MODELS LIMITED”), and the annex index is grouped under the same headings. Section 2 of that brief (item 34) adds review frequency and next review to Part A, a Supply chain Part after it, Companies House identity verification and PEP and adverse media rows for every director and PSC, one insurance row per policy (insurer, policy number, cover, expiry and the employer’s PAYE reference are held on the company record), and a Worker arrangements Part before the certificate. The broker cannot sign the pack until staff approve every PEP row and confirm every worker row, and rolled-up holiday pay is refused for regular-hours workers. The contract cover keeps the broker’s own title and puts the contract details in the cover’s text slots in the bottom-right quarter, never across the full width. Supplier: HMRC PAYE registration letter, Employers’ Liability certificate, and EL policy pages naming the supplier. The pack generates declarations and disclosures for each company, and a director’s certificate (HMRC standing and disclosures) that the **broker’s director** signs in the app while building the pack. Received and checked dates are entered on the contract, because they change with each client; they are set per company (one company’s documents are usually received a day or two before the other’s) and can be edited per document. Client name-change certificates are entered on the contract. A pack can be left half-built: uploads and ticks save at once, and typed details save with the sticky Save progress bar on the contract page and on company records (item 35). Compile the pack, then the broker signs it in the app. Send stays blocked until that signature. Then bake → send → the client signs. Only the client signs by link: the broker, contractor and supplier each have a saved signature on their record (their name typed in one of the handwriting fonts, or an uploaded image), and sending applies those, dated the day of sending, before the client is emailed. Send stays blocked until each saved signature is for the person named on the contract and the contractor and supplier have a director ID on their records. At issue the whole signed pack is added to the contract as an extra document, after the contract’s back cover. The client is looked up at Companies House for its contract details only (name, number, registered office, directors); nothing is fetched as a document for the client. Signing does not ask again for documents already in that pack. The client uploads its incorporation certificate, VAT certificate and director ID when it signs. The contractor’s and supplier’s director IDs are uploaded on their records (kept out of the pack) and bound into the signed contract. The broker uploads nothing. A signature in the signing room or on the pack can be drawn, typed in one of six bundled handwriting fonts, or uploaded as an image (paper background removed); a saved signature on a record is typed or uploaded. The broker’s pack signing page offers the broker’s saved signature. There is no audit trail for now (user decision, 26 Nov 2026): no audit pages on contracts, no signing line under the pack certificate, and no IP address, font name or signing method shown anywhere. IP addresses are not recorded for any party; never write a placeholder address.
5. **Access** — staff pages need an email and password. Sign links stay open to the party who holds the link; only the client is sent one. The broker opens the due diligence pack from the contract to sign it; that page is not emailed. The library, companies and contracts live in the server database, so every browser sees the same records.

## Stack

- Monorepo: `client/` (Vite React), `server/` (Express + `node:sqlite`).
- Data: `server/data/attest.sqlite`, files under `server/data/files/`.
- Mail: the compliance company SMTP (Google Workspace / Gmail App Password), or Outlook on Windows as a fallback. Brokers do not send. Outbound mail is still logged in `outbound_emails`.
- Prod-ish local: `npm run build && npm start` (API serves built UI on **8787**).

## Phone demo tunnel

- Cloudflare `*.trycloudflare.com` quick tunnels proved unreliable (HTTP 530 while process still “up”).
- Use `scripts/keep-tunnel-alive.sh` → **localhost.run** `*.lhr.life`.
- Live URL is written to `TUNNEL_URL.txt` (gitignored). Do not commit ephemeral URLs.
- **Persistence rule:** coding/commits must not restart the tunnel. Publishing to the live demo is explicit via `bash scripts/deploy-live.sh` (restarts API only; URL stays). One-time/recovery bring-up: `bash scripts/start-live.sh`.
- Watchdog must **not** recycle SSH while the local API is briefly down (deploys); only restart SSH if it died or public fails while local is healthy.
