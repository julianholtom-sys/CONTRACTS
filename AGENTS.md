# Attest — agent handoff

Read this file and `docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md` at the start of any session on this repo.
Update both when product decisions, architecture, or demo access change, then commit and push.

## Product intent

- E-sign for **Union Payroll**. Almost every contract is 3-way (**Contractor**, **Broker**, **Client**). A 4-way adds **Supplier (SPV)**. API keys remain `supplier` / `agency` / `company`, plus `spv`. Sign order is Contractor, Supplier (4-way only), Broker, Client.
- **Only the brokers are real so far** (Union Payroll, E Street Management, and their signatories). Every contractor, supplier and client record and every contract in the database is test data, including the live Companies House companies used for testing (MISSIONOPSAI LTD, ARC RISK MODELS LIMITED, DEVI MATA LTD). Never treat their signatories as real people: don't ask for their signatures or IDs or list them as to-dos.
- Spec: `docs/esign-data-model.pdf` (Postgres/GCS in the brief → SQLite + local disk here).
- The app header says **Contract Builder**. A contract is sent for a broker, and that broker’s cover is what the parties see on the document. Union Payroll (https://union-payroll.ltd/) is one broker. E Street Management (https://es.management/) is another. The header does not use a single broker’s logo. Accent `#0074FF`, Manrope, white-text primary buttons.

## Current product shape

1. **Brokers** — each broker holds legal details, the front cover, the back cover, and the brand SVG. A cover can be a template PDF with named text fields and placeholders such as `[Reference]`; the app fills and flattens it (`addTemplateCover`, generator `scripts/covers/es-management.mjs`). E Street Management’s covers work that way; Union Payroll’s are filled by layout detection. Brokers do not send email. They have no sending domain, from address, mailbox, VAT number, or signature. The **Compliance company** sends the contract; its introductory email names the broker it is sent on behalf of (`{{broker_name}}`, `{{compliance_name}}`, and `{{other_parties}}`, which follows 3-way or 4-way). The compliance company has no Companies House number. The due diligence pack is built and signed in the app, and is not emailed out for anyone to complete. The API still stores brokers as entities.
2. **Templates** — there are three contract types (user decision, 27 Nov 2026): **3-way lower**, **3-way higher** and **4-way** (`contract_type` = `three_lower`, `three_higher`, `four` on templates and contracts; `party_shape` still says `three` or `four` and drives parties, sign order and the pack, so both 3-way types have the 3-way parties and pack). Each broker has one contract per type and its own **schedules**; each schedule is ticked for any mix of the three types (`appendices.contract_types`, null = every type). They are uploaded on the Templates page (one section per broker) or under **Contracts and schedules** on the broker’s page. Until a broker’s contract for a type is uploaded, the shared placeholder master is used. The brokers’ real 3-way contracts and due diligence packs are Word files with `{{…}}` merge fields and a system legend (`docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md` item 50). They supply the wording only (user decision, 27 Nov 2026): the app fills them for each contract (records, Companies House, contractor fee, payment models, references, dates) and lays the wording out in its own style, like the 4-way pack (`server/src/docxContent.js` reads the Word body, `server/src/docLayout.js` lays it out), between the broker’s saved front and back covers. The Word files’ own covers, back pages and legend are left out, and Word is not used. Signature, date, name and position boxes come from the `{{sig.<party>.…}}` fields. The contract cover carries the reference (Doc ID) and the issue date, filled when it is sent. Lower and higher have the same parties and sign order; they differ in wording and in their due diligence pack (the higher pack asks for more HMRC and ownership evidence). On 3-way lower and higher the broker’s Parts A–K pack replaces the current pack, filled by staff in the app from Part A (its “How to use this pack” is left out); the contractor’s saved signature signs Part J at compile and the broker signs Part K in the app. It stays a separate PDF that the client gets with the completed contract. 4-way keeps the current pack. Same file controls as company brand files: **Open / Replace / Delete** (Are you sure?) plus **Add schedule**. Uploading a PDF detects Client / Broker / Contractor fields, and Supplier (SPV) when that word is on the page. Signing has jump-to.
3. **Start** — after login the only question is: working from the library, or sending a new contract? A new contract asks for the broker (from the saved list), the contract type — 3-way lower, 3-way higher or 4-way (which picks that broker’s contract) — the schedules (that broker’s schedules for the type, all ticked to start), then the other parties (company, signatory, role, email). The library path picks a saved broker and contractor, then asks the contract type, the schedules and the remaining parties. Companies and templates stay available from a setup link, not the main navigation. Every party record stores company name, signatory, role (Director or Authorised signatory) and email. The contract carries a signature and a date for each party. The broker signs and dates the due diligence pack.
4. **Contracts** (API still uses envelope tables/routes; UI always says contract) — the due diligence pack is built in the app before the contract is issued. No party is emailed to complete it. Companies House filings (incorporation, change of name, registered office, PSC, officers) and a UK Sanctions List check are fetched from the company number for the contractor and, on a 4-way, the supplier. Only one filing per fact is kept (the incorporation, each change of name, the latest AD01, notices for current PSCs only) and the pack attaches only its first page, the certificate or notice — never the IN01, model articles or PSC history. Staff attach only what cannot be fetched. Contractor: VAT certificate, Employers’ Liability, Public Liability, Professional Indemnity certificate, PI policy schedule and wording, bank statement for the account paying workers and HMRC, illustrative payslip, and a payroll chain map with PAYE/ERN confirmation. Pack and app wording stays short: checklists, not explanations. The pack is laid out like the worked example: the broker’s front cover with the pack details in its panel, branded headers, page numbers and the pack reference on every page (never “Doc ID”), bordered tables, check results Clear (green tick) / Flag (red cross) / N/A with “Reviewed by / Date” under each checks table, annexes numbered 1–n and titled “Annex n: Document, DD/MM/YYYY”, a Disclosures Part listing every Companies House change, and framed, captioned annex pages (`server/src/packLayout.js`). The v7 brief (`docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md` item 31) was written for the 4-way pack; by user decision its fixes apply to 3-way packs and the contract covers too, and its section 2 additions get a version without the SPV for 3-way packs. Each company’s annexes start with a divider page headed like its Part (e.g. “Part D — Supplier (SPV): ARC RISK MODELS LIMITED”), and the annex index is grouped under the same headings. Section 2 of that brief (item 34) adds review frequency and next review to Part A, a Supply chain Part after it, Companies House identity verification and PEP and adverse media rows for every director and PSC, one insurance row per contractor policy (insurer, policy number, cover, expiry and the employer’s PAYE reference are held on the company record), and a Worker arrangements Part before the certificate. The broker cannot sign the pack until staff approve every PEP row and confirm every worker row, and rolled-up holiday pay is refused for regular-hours workers. The contract cover keeps the broker’s own title and puts the contract details in the cover’s text slots in the bottom-right quarter, never across the full width. Supplier: HMRC PAYE registration letter only. The supplier has no insurance of its own: as in the worked example, it is added to the contractor’s Employers’ Liability policy, so on a 4-way the contractor’s record carries the EL certificate naming the supplier and the policy pages adding it, and the supplier’s EL row in the pack reads the contractor’s policy (user decision, 26 Nov 2026). The pack generates declarations and disclosures for each company, and a director’s certificate (HMRC standing and disclosures) that the **broker’s director** signs in the app while building the pack. Received and checked dates are entered on the contract, because they change with each client; they are set per company (one company’s documents are usually received a day or two before the other’s) and can be edited per document. A sanctions search is dated the day the broker checked it, not the day the app ran it. Ask for or attach only documents that appear in the worked example (`DD-Example.pdf`), the user’s 24 Nov 2026 list (director IDs; the client’s incorporation, VAT and director ID), the 25 Nov 2026 checklist, or the v7 brief (which keeps the supplier’s Companies House filings and sanctions search as annexes); anything else needs the user’s say-so (user decision, 26 Nov 2026). No name-change certificates are asked of the client. A pack can be left half-built: uploads and ticks save at once, and typed details save with the sticky Save progress bar on the contract page and on company records (item 35). Compile the pack, then the broker signs it in the app. Send stays blocked until that signature. Then bake → send → the client signs. Only the client signs by link: the contractor, the supplier (4-way) and the broker each have a saved signature on their record (their name typed in one of the handwriting fonts, or an uploaded image), and sending applies those in sign order (Contractor, Supplier, Broker), each dated as entered under **Dates signed** when the contract is issued (in sign order, not before the prepared date, not after today), before the client is emailed and signs last. Send stays blocked until each saved signature is for the person named on the contract and the contractor and supplier have a director ID on their records. At issue the whole signed pack is added to the contract as an extra document, after the contract’s back cover. People read, then sign: the contract’s page with the signature fields is placed after the contract terms and the schedules, immediately before the back cover (user decision, 26 Nov 2026); the only signature elsewhere is the broker’s on the pack. A broker’s own contract gets only the prepared/issued dates line stamped on it (its branding is its own); its detail boxes are filled from the records (the broker from its legal details, a missing registered office or number from Companies House), and Send stays blocked while a contractor, supplier or broker box can’t be filled, because only the client types into boxes. On a 4-way with personal guarantees, the Guarantor Director is always the company’s signatory (user decision, 26 Nov 2026): the label takes the signatory’s name and the saved signature also signs the guarantee. Documents bound in at signing (director IDs, the client’s certificates) come last, styled like the pack’s annexes: an index page, then each document captioned and framed under the broker’s header, with page numbers and the contract reference. The client is looked up at Companies House for its contract details only (name, number, registered office, directors); nothing is fetched as a document for the client. Signing does not ask again for documents already in that pack. The client uploads its incorporation certificate, VAT certificate and director ID when it signs. The contractor’s and supplier’s director IDs are uploaded on their records (kept out of the pack) and bound into the signed contract. The broker uploads nothing. A signature in the signing room or on the pack can be drawn, typed in one of six bundled handwriting fonts, or uploaded as an image (paper background removed); a saved signature on a record is typed or uploaded. The broker’s pack signing page offers the broker’s saved signature. There is no audit trail for now (user decision, 26 Nov 2026): no audit pages on contracts, no signing line under the pack certificate, and no IP address, font name or signing method shown anywhere. IP addresses are not recorded for any party; never write a placeholder address.
5. **Access** — staff pages need an email and password. Sign links stay open to the party who holds the link; only the client is sent one. The broker opens the due diligence pack from the contract to sign it; that page is not emailed. The library, companies and contracts live in the server database, so every browser sees the same records.

## Stack

- Monorepo: `client/` (Vite React), `server/` (Express + `node:sqlite`).
- Data: `server/data/attest.sqlite`, files under `server/data/files/`.
- Mail: the compliance company SMTP (Google Workspace / Gmail App Password), or Outlook on Windows as a fallback. Brokers do not send. Outbound mail is still logged in `outbound_emails`.
- Prod-ish local: `npm run build && npm start` (API serves built UI on **8787**).

## Phone demo tunnel

**Local only for now (user decision, 27 Nov 2026).** The app runs on the user’s Windows PC (`npm start`, http://localhost:8787). The localhost.run demo below ran on a separate Linux host that this PC cannot reach; don’t try to deploy it. When hosting is picked up again, the user prefers Hostinger: see `docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md` item 48 for what to check there and what the app needs first. Vercel doesn’t fit (no lasting disk, 4.5 MB file cap).

- Cloudflare `*.trycloudflare.com` quick tunnels proved unreliable (HTTP 530 while process still “up”).
- Use `scripts/keep-tunnel-alive.sh` → **localhost.run** `*.lhr.life`.
- Live URL is written to `TUNNEL_URL.txt` (gitignored). Do not commit ephemeral URLs.
- **Persistence rule:** coding/commits must not restart the tunnel. Publishing to the live demo is explicit via `bash scripts/deploy-live.sh` (restarts API only; URL stays). One-time/recovery bring-up: `bash scripts/start-live.sh`.
- Watchdog must **not** recycle SSH while the local API is briefly down (deploys); only restart SSH if it died or public fails while local is healthy.
